# POLARIS Design Direction

## Three Visual Approaches

### 1. Orbital Editorial
**Very Brief Intro:** A cinematic polar-Earth vista is paired with rigorous editorial typography and instrument-like data markings. The result feels like a research journal projected through satellite mission control.

**Probability:** 0.037

### 2. Glacial Field Notes
**Very Brief Intro:** Pale ice surfaces, black field-journal annotations, and documentary photography create a tactile research-outpost atmosphere. The experience is calm, scientific, and human-scaled.

## Chosen Direction — Orbital Editorial

### Design Movement
**Orbital Editorial** blends 1970s space-program technical graphics with contemporary science-journal art direction. It frames POLARIS as an active observatory rather than a generic digital product.

### Core Principles
1. **Cinematic science:** Earth, ice, and atmosphere provide scale, while clean data marks establish scientific credibility.
2. **Purposeful asymmetry:** Content sits on an off-centre reading axis, opposed by orbital visual information and geographic annotation.
3. **Measured luminosity:** Cyan is used as an instrument signal against ink-dark navy, never as decoration without meaning.
4. **Quiet complexity:** Fine grids, coordinate labels, and contour lines reward attention without obstructing reading.

### Color Philosophy
The base is **midnight blue-black**, inspired by the sky beyond the atmosphere, to create space and seriousness. Cool white preserves editorial clarity. A restrained ice-cyan signal, **Aurora Cyan #84EFF5**, identifies live data, paths, and calls to action, while muted slate captures secondary structure. Light is concentrated around the polar Earth and data paths so that illumination always suggests discovery.

### Layout Paradigm
The landing page is organized as a **vertical expedition descent**: the cinematic orbital view establishes global scope; a narrow signal bar records the platform’s reach; broad geographic spreads invite polar exploration; and editorial research, route maps, and learning pathways progressively bring the visitor closer to the people and knowledge inside the system. The primary reading column is deliberately left-weighted; visual telemetry occupies the right edge.

### Signature Elements
- **Orbit threads:** restrained dotted and solid curved trajectories that imply satellites, navigation, and linked knowledge.
- **Latitude capsules:** compact uppercase geographic labels with longitude/latitude-style dividers.
- **Ice grain:** a low-contrast granular field overlay that adds depth to darker surfaces and never competes with text.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should resemble operating an instrument: direct, calm, and legible. Links respond with a precise cyan trace; cards lift by a few pixels as if rising through an observation plane; navigation is immediate and anchors smoothly to content. All preview-only controls honestly signal that a future portal flow would open.

### Animation
The hero Earth uses a very slow cinematic drift, with non-essential orbital paths and point lights moving on long, offset cycles. Section elements reveal vertically with 40–70ms staggers and a crisp deceleration. Hover and button feedback stays within 160–220ms. Motion only uses opacity and transforms where feasible, and all atmospheric animation is reduced when a visitor requests reduced motion.

### Typography System
**Space Grotesk** drives headlines, navigational labels, figures, and navigation because its geometric construction echoes aerospace graphics. **DM Sans** handles descriptive copy for its compact readability. Headlines use tightly tracked, confident uppercase text at major moments; section titles use mixed case where editorial warmth helps. Metadata uses a fine, wide-tracked uppercase register.

### Brand Essence
**POLARIS is the science gateway for anyone who wants to see, connect, and understand a changing polar world.**

**Personality:** precise, expansive, lucid.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are declarative and observant rather than promotional. CTAs invite investigation instead of promising outcomes. Microcopy should sound like a field note: factual, spare, and directional.

> “The poles are not remote. They are connected to every horizon.”

> “Trace the evidence across ice, ocean, orbit, and time.”

### Wordmark & Logo
The wordmark is a wide, technical **POLARIS** lockup with carefully spaced geometry. The mark is a bold north-star aperture: an eight-point radial instrument symbol with one elongated polar axis, rendered as a solid ice-cyan graphic on transparent background.

### Signature Brand Color
**Aurora Cyan — #84EFF5**

## Style Decisions

- Every section carries a visible orbital-editorial device: a coordinate rule, archive number, route trace, instrument readout, latitude capsule, or field-note label.
- The cyan north-star aperture recurs as the primary POLARIS glyph. Aurora Cyan is reserved for actions, live signals, paths, and important evidence markers.
- Research and learning modules use archive and field-note hierarchy so they read as evidence records rather than generic marketing cards.
- All portal routes inherit a midnight command bar, POLARIS aperture glyph, coordinate field, and Aurora Cyan status/action signal so the operating environment is recognisably one observatory.
- Form, dashboard, moderation, and empty states retain archive metadata and telemetry motifs rather than reverting to default application surfaces.
- Access surfaces behave as observatory **access ledgers**: a readable credential sheet carries coordinate rules, archive numbers, mode labels, and mission-status framing rather than looking like a generic login card.
- Aurora Cyan is used only for active access mode, verified access, orbital paths, coordinates, and primary action states; inactive controls remain cool white and muted slate.
- Role selection is expressed as an instrument mode—each choice is numbered, factual, and part of the same polar observation workflow.
- Access entry uses factual **access mode** and credential language rather than generic workspace language; every polar image includes a compact observation state, coordinate, or verified-evidence marker without decorative clutter.
- The access-page visual field is deliberately free of decorative orbit lines: the slow moving ice image, one coordinate anchor, and a compact evidence readout carry the observation story.
